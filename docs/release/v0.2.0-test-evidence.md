## OPPO PMA120 physical-device QA

All direct UI taps were followed by a five-second response wait. The exact candidate above remains installed on the device in the final safe state: System default language, report redaction on, 30-second refresh, and Android Wi-Fi scan throttling on.

### Upgrade, identity, and launch

- The official v0.1.0 APK was installed first and its permanent signing certificate verified. Privacy mode and a 60-second refresh preference were set before upgrade.
- `adb install -r` installed v0.2.0 without uninstalling. `firstInstallTime` remained `2026-08-02 12:22:45`; the retained privacy and 60-second preferences demonstrated migration; package and signer identities remained unchanged.
- Pulling the installed `base.apk` produced 2,025,741 bytes and SHA-256 `15e690c0314feee3a5a2d925f676aed2e9887336f57d8f590ca9868179dd542c`, byte-identical to the candidate.
- Final package-manager state reports version 2 / 0.2.0, min 26, target 36, no `INTERNET`, and granted coarse/precise location. A forced final launch returned `LaunchState: COLD` in 119 ms with no matching fatal exception or ANR in the inspected log.

### Throttle eligibility and measured cadence

- With the actual ColorOS Developer options **Wi-Fi scan throttling** switch on, five seconds was visible, disabled, and exposed the localized accessibility explanation.
- Turning that switch off made five seconds selectable. A redacted foreground CSV trace ran for 155,013 ms and contained 33 records. Every record reported `request_accepted=true`, `results_updated=true`, and `likely_throttled=false`.
- Excluding the initial/startup observations, 31 consecutive deltas had minimum 4,877 ms, median 5,004 ms, mean 4,999.8 ms, and maximum 5,138 ms. Source age across the trace had minimum 163 ms, median 202 ms, and maximum 5,190 ms; after startup, observed ages were at most 429 ms.
- The trace contained only scan flags, source age, record number, and elapsed time; it was redacted and contained no AP identifiers. Its exact CSV preview was opened. Share opened Android's chooser with a read-granted, non-exported cache `FileProvider` URI; no external recipient was selected.
- Re-enabling the real Developer option made the app report throttling on, immediately fall back to 30 seconds, and disable five seconds. The underlying global override was returned to its original absent/default state. Two unrelated Developer-option switches changed during coordinate validation were detected immediately, restored off, and rechecked.

These measurements show this one OPPO delivered fresh results close to the five-second request cadence during this run. They do not guarantee the same cadence on another chipset, OEM build, regulatory domain, or radio environment.

### Lifecycle, manual refresh, reset, and privacy

- Pause held the Track sample count at 27 for 15 seconds while source age increased from 10 to 25 seconds. Resume advanced the count to 28 and then 30 after six seconds, with fresh source age.
- During a 20-second Home/background interval, the sample count did not exhibit periodic five-second foreground cadence; returning to the app resumed acquisition. This is bounded UI evidence, supplemented by deterministic lifecycle/scheduler tests.
- Process restart and manual refresh completed without crash or ANR. Timeout recovery and exact same-in-flight manual coalescing remain deterministic JVM evidence; no physical timeout was injected.
- Reset restored privacy preferences, report redaction, 30-second refresh, and the empty System-default locale override. A final cold start retained that state.
- Attempting to disable report redaction displayed the full localized warning naming SSID, BSSID, local IP address, gateway, DNS, and precise timestamp exposure. Choosing **Keep redaction on** left the toggle checked. Automated instrumentation separately covers accepting the warning and the dedicated unredacted preview/share action; raw identifiers were intentionally not exported during this physical run.
- The short run produced no crash, ANR, or visible thermal/performance problem. Two minutes is insufficient to quantify battery impact, so no battery-life claim is made.

## Truthfulness assertions

- Five seconds becomes selectable only after Android explicitly reports Wi-Fi scan throttling off.
- An enabled or unavailable capability disables it; a saved five-second value is held during discovery, then persisted and rescheduled to 30 seconds if discovery ends in an ineligible state.
- At most one request is in flight. Late completion cannot overlap the next request, manual refresh is coalesced, and a 15-second timeout permits recovery.
- Leaving the foreground or pausing stops fast requests; returning or resuming restarts only the one application coordinator.
- Only a successful updated broadcast with advancing platform timestamps, or a successful newly empty result, advances snapshot sequence/capture time and downstream history/logging.
- Rejected, explicitly non-updated, unchanged-timestamp, cached, and timed-out cycles retain the previous observation identity while updating request flags and connection evidence.
- `likelyThrottled` reflects the latest request cycle and remains distinct from Android's configured throttle switch.
